Last game · Friday, Nov. 6 · Smoothie King Center

MIN 115NOP 109Loss

New Orleans led 79–77 after three quarters and lost the fourth 38–30. Jeremiah Fears had his best scoring night of the season, 24 points on 9-of-16 shooting, and Trey Murphy III returned from his knee issue with 10 points and 5 assists. Zion Williamson was held to 13 on 10 shots.

The Pelicans did a lot right: 10 of 24 from three, 30 fast-break points, only 13 turnovers. What sank them was the glass and the rim. Minnesota grabbed 17 offensive rebounds for 20 second-chance points, shot 23 of 32 at the rim, and got 11 more free-throw attempts. Herb Jones fouled out.

What the numbers say

Four patterns from the first nine games. Everything here is computed from the full team box scores.

+39 / −27

Third quarters win games, fourth quarters lose them

In the six regulation games, New Orleans outscored opponents by 39 in third quarters and got outscored by 27 in fourth quarters. Add 1–4 in games decided by six points or fewer, and late-game execution is the clearest gap between 4–5 and 6–3.

−84

Points lost at the three-point line

Opponents have made 28 more threes, worth 84 points. The Pelicans win the paint by 72 and the free-throw line by 33, which almost exactly cancels it. That’s why their point differential is just +13 despite a top-tier offense.

Opponents’ shooting at the rim

The defense forces turnovers (11.8 steals a game) but almost never stops a shot near the basket. Opponents convert better than 7 of every 10 attempts at the rim, and New Orleans has no rim protector in the rotation with Yves Missi out.

+15.3

Bench scoring margin per game

The second unit averages 46.1 points to opponents’ 30.8. Saddiq Bey, Trendon Watford and Jordan Poole have made depth a real strength, which is part of why the regular-season ceiling looks higher than the preseason projections.
